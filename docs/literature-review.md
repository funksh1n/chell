My project explores combining the convenience of a modern smartphone with the form and aesthetics of a traditional flip phone. Traditional flip phones are compact and visually distinctive, but their physical keypads make typing slower and their software often cannot support many modern smartphone applications. My proposed device will use two physical touchscreens joined by a hinge. The screens will function as one continuous interface rather than as two independent monitors, allowing modern smartphone content and interaction to extend across both halves. The idea was partly inspired by seeing text and other content span the two screens of the Nintendo 3DS.
Yang et al. study dual-display phones and investigate how multiple displays can create new interaction possibilities. Their work demonstrates that a second display can be meaningfully incorporated into mobile interaction, but their device places displays on opposite sides of a phone rather than using two displays as the two halves of a clamshell. [1]
Yeh, Irudayaraj, and Vogel study a modified clamshell phone with a flexible touchscreen. Their research demonstrates interactions that use both the touchscreen and the phone's folding structure. This is relevant to my project because it shows how the clamshell form can be part of the interface, but their device relies on one flexible display rather than two separate displays. [2]
The Samsung Galaxy Z Flip demonstrates that a modern smartphone can be placed into a compact clamshell form. However, it uses a flexible display that folds and unfolds into a conventional smartphone screen. [3]
The gap I intend to explore is a clamshell smartphone built around two physical displays rather than a single folding display. Instead of adapting an existing smartphone to fold, I will build a device designed around the flip-phone form from the beginning. The project will produce a working physical prototype and the software needed to demonstrate its functionality.
